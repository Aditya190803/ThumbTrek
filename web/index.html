How it works

A trek, not a guilt trip

ThumbTrek measures distance, not doom. No blocking, no lectures — just the map of where your thumb already went.

Grant Accessibility once

The service listens for scroll events in the apps you choose. It never asks for permission to read screen content, so it cannot see posts, text or URLs — only how far a view moved.

Scroll like you already do

Pixels become metres using your screen's density: pixels ÷ dpi × 0.0254. Nothing to start, nothing to stop, no timer running in the corner.

Check the board when you want

Daily and weekly totals, streaks, personal records, shareable cards, and friend or global boards that stay empty until you opt in.

The scale

Metres mean nothing. Landmarks do.

A number like 830 m doesn't land. So every total gets measured against something you can picture — the ladder the app actually walks up.

Design

Built for the long scroll

Local-first storage, signed sideloaded updates, and a social layer that stays quiet until you turn it on.

Self-updating APK

Checks GitHub Releases over Wi‑Fi, verifies the SHA‑256, then asks once to install. No Play Store required.

Opt-in social

Friend codes, weekly boards, anonymous handles. Nothing leaves the phone until you say so.

Honest numbers

No calibration slider on competitive scores. A slider would just be an honour-system cheat dial — everyone measures the same raw trek.

Install

A minute, once

Grab the latest signed APK from GitHub Releases. Allow installs from your browser once, and ThumbTrek handles every update after that.

Privacy

What we see — and don't

Nothing leaves the phone until you turn leaderboards on. After that, two different things do — and they have two different audiences.

Never read

No screenshots, no feed text, no URLs. The service never requests content access — it reads the scroll numbers the event itself carries and nothing else.

Published to the board

A name you choose — your Google one or an anonymous handle — and three distances: this week, this month, all time. Anyone signed in can read those. That is what a leaderboard is.

Synced privately to you

Your day-by-day history and per-app split, readable by your account and nobody else's. That private copy is what lets the dashboard chart your history and what makes it survive a reinstall.

Either way the history lives on your phone first, and exports as CSV from Settings. Turning the leaderboard off deletes both halves from the server.
