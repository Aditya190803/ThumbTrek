It counts distance, and nothing else

No blocking and no lectures. ThumbTrek shows how far your thumb already went, and one full lap of the track is your daily limit.

  1. 1

    Grant Accessibility once

    The service listens for scroll events in the apps you choose. It never asks for permission to read screen content, so it cannot see posts, text or URLs. It only sees how far a view moved.

  2. 2

    Scroll like you already do

    Pixels become metres using your screen's density: pixels ÷ dpi × 0.0254. There is nothing to start and nothing to stop.

  3. 3

    Pick a limit, or don't

    Set a daily distance, and every day you finish at or under it adds to your clean-day run. To just watch instead, baseline mode keeps the numbers with no cap and no verdicts.

  4. 4

    Check the board when you want

    Daily and weekly totals, streaks, personal records, shareable cards, and friend or global leaderboards that stay empty until you opt in.

On your phone and in your browser

Updates itself

The app checks GitHub Releases over Wi‑Fi, verifies the SHA‑256, then asks once to install. No Play Store required.

Desktop counts too

A browser extension measures the same feeds on the web. Sign in on both and they add up on one dashboard.

Honest numbers

There is no calibration slider on leaderboard scores, because a slider would be an honour-system cheat dial. Everyone is measured the same way.

What ThumbTrek sees, and what it doesn't

Nothing leaves the phone until you turn leaderboards on. After that, two different things do, and they have two different audiences.

Never read

No screenshots, no feed text, no URLs. The service never requests content access. It reads the scroll numbers the event itself carries and nothing else.

Published to the board

A name you choose, either your Google one or an anonymous handle, and three distances: this week, this month, all time. Anyone signed in can read those, because that is what a leaderboard is.

Synced privately to you

Your day-by-day history and per-app split, readable by your account and nobody else's. That private copy lets the dashboard chart your history and lets it survive a reinstall.

Either way the history lives on your phone first, and exports as CSV from Settings. Turning the leaderboard off deletes both halves from the server.
