Privacy

Last updated 20 November 2026

ThumbTrek measures how far you scroll. This page says exactly what it collects, where that goes, who can read it, and how to remove it. It covers the Android app, the browser extension and this website.

What is measured, and what is not

  • The Android app reads the scroll offsets that Android's accessibility events carry for the apps you choose, and nothing else. It never reads text, images, links or anything else on screen, and it takes no screenshots.
  • The extension reads one number per scroll on the sites you switch on, and the site's name. It reads no page content. Sites you have not switched on are discarded before anything is stored.
  • What is kept is a distance per app or site per day, and your settings. That stays on your device.

What leaves your device

Nothing, until you sign in and turn on leaderboards. Signing in alone publishes nothing. After you turn leaderboards on, three kinds of data are stored in Google Firebase:

  • Your board row. A name you choose (your Google name or an anonymous handle) and three distances: this week, this month and all time. Anyone signed in can read it, because that is what a leaderboard is.
  • Your private history. Your distance per day, split by app or site. Only your account can read it. It lets the dashboard draw your history.
  • What you share on purpose. Friends see your real Google name. Groups you join show your name and your distance inside the group's window to the other members. If you turn on one limit across your devices, the limit is stored in your account, readable only by you.

Who processes it

Data you publish is stored in Google Firebase (Firestore and Authentication). Sign-in uses your Google account. The Android app downloaded from GitHub checks GitHub for updates with an anonymous request that carries no account, no device identifier and no scroll data. The Google Play version gets its updates from Google Play and makes no such request. There is no advertising, no analytics and no tracking of any kind, and nothing is sold.

When the Android app crashes, it sends a crash report to Google Firebase Crashlytics so the bug can be found and fixed. A report holds the stack trace, the device model, the Android version and the app version. It holds no scroll data, no app or site names you track and nothing from your screen, and it is not tied to your account. Crashlytics keeps reports for 90 days.

Backups and exports

Backup files and CSV exports are written to a place you choose and are never uploaded by ThumbTrek. Keep them somewhere safe: they hold your full history.

Deleting your data

  • Deleting your account removes everything above from the server in one step: your board row, your private history, your friends, your groups and your shared limit. You can do it in the Android app or on the dashboard, and Delete your account explains how, what is kept, and what to do if you can no longer sign in.
  • Turning leaderboards off deletes your board row and your private history from the server, and takes your numbers out of every group.
  • Leaving a group removes your numbers from it. Deleting a group you own removes everyone's numbers from it.
  • Uninstalling the app or removing the extension deletes everything stored on that device.

Payments

Premium is not on sale yet, and ThumbTrek takes no payments. When it does, payment details will be handled by the payment provider and never stored by ThumbTrek. This page will be updated before that happens.

Children

ThumbTrek is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.

Questions

Open an issue on GitHub. The source code is public, so every claim on this page can be checked.
